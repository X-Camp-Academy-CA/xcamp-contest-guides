📋 Contest Format

Date: Sunday, June 14, 2026 — in-person at The College of New Jersey (TCNJ) + online mirror

Schedule: Check-in 9:30 AM → Opening 10:30 AM → Contest: 11:00 AM–2:00 PM (3 hours) → Discussion 2:00 PM → Closing 2:45 PM

Practice: Online practice contest on June 13 (day before) — test the environment & get a feel for the problem types.

Teams: Up to 3 members per team, working on a single laptop.

Scoring: USACO-style. Ranked by number of problems solved. Problems range from AP CSA to USACO Platinum level.

Languages: C++11/17/20/23  |  C11  |  Java 8/11/17/21  |  Python 3.12.3 (Python may TLE on harder problems)

Rules: Standard I/O. Any pre-published resource allowed; no generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT). No internet during contest.

Location: Room 115, Education Building, TCNJ (in-person) — or anywhere (online).

Prizes: Over $1,000 prize pool including top 3 team prizes and raffle prizes (in-person only eligible for prizes).

💡 How to Prepare — By Your Current X-Camp Level

Click your level to expand recommendations:

📘 CS101P / CS102P — Python foundations ▶
  • Start with Novice Division. Target Problem A (Punching Wood) — basic math is all you need!
  • Problem B (The Climb to the Top) is a great stretch goal once you're comfortable with greedy thinking.
  • Practice: solve easy problems on Codeforces (800–1000 rating) to build speed and confidence.
📗 CS200 / CS201 / CS202 — Recursion, DFS, BFS ▶
  • Target Novice A–C. Problem B uses greedy + simulation; Problem C uses prefix/suffix reasoning.
  • Problem D (Subdricate) is a stretch goal — requires sorting + prefix sum patterns from CS300.
  • Focus on implementing clean solutions quickly. Speed matters in a 3-hour contest!
📙 CS300 / CS301 / CS302 — Silver-level algorithms ▶
  • Target Novice A–E solidly. D (Subdricate) and E (Parallel Sort) are prime Silver problems.
  • Problem F (Divisible Subset) and G (Lake) are accessible stretch goals after finishing CS302.
  • Consider registering for Advanced Division if you've completed CS302 — Adv A–B are within reach.
📕 CS400H / CS401H / CS501 — Gold-level prep ▶
  • Register for Advanced Division. Aim for Adv A–D (shared Novice E–H problems).
  • Problem B (Divisible Subset) directly uses CS501 number theory. Problem D (Can YOU Sort It?) uses CS400H/401H constructive skills.
  • Adv E (Circular Permutation) is a strong stretch goal combining graph theory + DP.
📓 CS500H / CS502 — Platinum track ▶
  • Register for Advanced Division. Push for Adv A–F.
  • Adv E (Circular Permutation) and F (Hidden Maxima) need advanced DP + combinatorics.
  • Adv G–H require techniques from CS602/603 — keep them as long-term goals.
🏆 CS602 / CS603 — US Camp / USACO Platinum ▶
  • All Advanced problems are fair game. Adv G (Butterfly Effect) and H (TPXGMQ) are your battleground.
  • Top 3 overall placement and gift cards are a realistic goal at this level.
  • TPXGMQ has a generous 15-second time limit — complex implementations are expected.

📌 Note on Difficulty Levels: The USACO equivalent ratings shown above are cumulative — each level builds on all previous ones. A Silver problem assumes comfort with Bronze-level skills (loops, arrays, basic math), and a Gold problem assumes both Bronze and Silver knowledge. Novice E–H and Advanced A–D are the same problems — if you're between divisions, try both!
💡 Division Overlap: WWPIT's two-division structure means the top 4 Novice problems (E–H) are identical to the bottom 4 Advanced problems (A–D). Students comfortable at the Bronze–Silver boundary should consider trying both divisions to maximize their experience.
